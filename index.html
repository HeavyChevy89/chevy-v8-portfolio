About this build

The goal was an all-original concept engine, not a copy of an existing design: a 90° V8 with a cross-plane crank and a 4-valve SOHC valvetrain, built with performance in mind — large head port volume, aggressive cam specs, and individual throttle bodies for both throttle response and looks. The firing order (1-3-7-2-6-5-4-8) was chosen specifically for better vibration balance versus the other options available on a cross-plane V8. Every major system — block, heads, crank, main caps, oil pan, and intake — was modeled as its own part file and brought together into one top-level assembly, the way a real production engine is organized rather than as a single solid.

The hardest part of the whole project was the intake and exhaust porting on the cylinder head — it took about two and a half weeks to make any real progress, plus another week just deciding on the final port shape. Water pump, oil pump, and the hard tubing for both systems are still on the to-do list. Going in, I only had a loose grasp of thermal expansion in an engine context; coming out of it, I have a much better feel for realistic allowances and tolerancing.
